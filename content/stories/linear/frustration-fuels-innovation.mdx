# The Birth of Linear: When Frustration Fuels Innovation

In 2019, Jori Lallo and Ville Vänni were drowning in chaotic project management tools.

After years at Rovio working on Angry Birds, they'd experienced firsthand how clunky, slow issue trackers destroyed team productivity. Existing solutions felt like "digital hoarders"—packed with features nobody used but impossible to navigate.

The turning point came during a late-night coding session. Ville complained about spending more time fighting Jira than writing code. Jori replied, "What if we built something that just works?" That simple question sparked a radical idea: strip away everything unnecessary and focus purely on speed and clarity.

They quit their jobs and began building in secret. Their philosophy was brutal: if a feature didn't directly help teams ship faster, it didn't belong. They obsessed over micro-interactions—keyboard shortcuts, instant search, seamless transitions. Every pixel served a purpose.

Early users were skeptical. "Another issue tracker?" But when they tried Linear, the difference was immediate. Teams reported cutting meeting time by 30% because everyone could instantly see what was happening. The waitlist grew to 50,000 before public launch.

Today, Linear powers product teams at Google, Uber, and OpenAI—not because it has more features, but because it has fewer distractions.

> "Great software isn't about what you add. It's about what you have the courage to remove." — Jori Lallo, CEO of Linear
